# Canonical case source inventory

This inventory records the provenance and unresolved boundaries of the three
`plimsoll-project-1` fixtures generated by
`tools/plimsoll/tools/gen_project_cases.py`. The Queen Mary fixture is a
calculable engineering scenario, not a recovered historical weight ledger or a
historical certification. Its nominal totals are not fitted to a published
displacement.

## HMS Queen Mary (1913)

### Historical and technical anchors

The bounded Task 3 audit adopted these anchors:

- Admiral Jellicoe, *The Grand Fleet 1914-1916*, Tables II and V, gives a Lion
  class designed load displacement of 26,350 tons, fuel capacity of 4,600 tons,
  and a Queen Mary comparison of 27,000 tons displacement with 3,900 tons
  "armour" and 2,300 tons "deck protection". The text does not settle the ton
  convention or the protection boundary. The raw figures therefore remain
  non-additive comparison metadata. [Primary text, pp. 308-312](https://readingroo.ms/7/5/3/2/75323/75323-h/75323-h.htm#Page_308)
- C. S. McDowell, "Notes on Inspection Duty at Shipbuilding Works",
  *U.S. Naval Institute Proceedings* (1912), gives an approximate screening
  rule of 15 horsepower per ton of machinery for heavy turbine vessels. The
  project applies it to 75,000 shp and converts an assumed long ton exactly,
  producing 5,080.234544 t. This is a cross-navy screening estimate, not a
  Queen Mary machinery schedule. [Primary technical article](https://www.usni.org/magazines/proceedings/1912/december-0/notes-inspection-duty-shipbuilding-works)
- J. J. Welch, *A Text Book of Naval Architecture* (HMSO, 1891), pp. 79-80,
  gives 30-40% of displacement as the hull material weight of the largest iron
  armour-clads. The project uses a 35% midpoint against an assumed-long-ton
  27,000-ton comparison, with a deliberately wider 25-42% applicability range.
  The result is 9,601.64328816 t. [Primary Admiralty textbook](https://upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/8/85/A_text_book_of_naval_architecture_-_for_the_use_of_officers_of_the_Royal_Navy_%28IA_cu31924105726123%29.pdf)
- Admiralty Gunnery Branch G.8652/13, *Handbook for the 4-inch Mark VII and
  VIII B.L. Guns* (1913, corrected September 1913), gives 42 cwt for the Mark
  VII gun, a 31 lb projectile, and a 9 lb 5 15/16 oz charge. These values anchor
  the secondary gun and per-round masses. The 150-round outfit remains an
  inherited secondary-source lead. [Handbook transcription](https://www.gwpda.org/naval/hb4_inch.htm),
  [archive catalogue identifier ADM 186/207](https://dc-naval-history.com/admiralty-publications.html)
- The existing NavWeaps case values supply 167,776 lb per 13.5-inch gun without
  breech, 600 tons for a Mark II mounting, 1,400 lb projectiles, 297 lb service
  charges, and 80 rounds per installed gun. The listed Queen Mary Mark II*
  mounting mass is unavailable. All uses remain estimates, and the intended
  complete gun boundary still has an unresolved breech contribution.
  [Secondary compilation](https://www.navweaps.com/Weapons/WNBR_135-45_mk5.php)

The inherited 26,770 and 31,650 displacement figures are retained verbatim as
"tons (unresolved)". Neither is assigned a canonical metric-tonne reference.
The 27,000-ton Jellicoe comparison also keeps its raw value; its metric value is
shown only as an explicitly assumed-long-ton comparison.

### Geometry and positions

The self-contained section polygons are materialized from the tracked
`tools/plimsoll/cases/queen_mary_1913_offsets.json` input through the existing
offset interpolator. That source is an estimated model table rather than a
historical lines plan. The polygons retain the model waterline datum and declare
`keel_offset_m = -9.9`; a canonical weight KG is therefore shifted into geometry
coordinates by adding the keel offset.

The tracked `queen_mary_v3/object_manifest.json` supplies estimated role and
object centres only. Its axes are converted as follows:

```text
canonical x_m  = model Y centre
canonical y_m  = model X centre
canonical kg_m = model Z centre + 9.9
```

The manifest never supplies mass. Hull, outfit, stores, water and torpedo
centres without a defensible role object use declared engineering centres with
wide position ranges. Compartment and opening coordinates are numerical
engineering fixtures; they are not surveyed subdivision or opening data.
Each project has an explicit intact damage preset with zero initial water in
every modeled compartment. Flood water is scenario state and is not included as
a permanent solid weight.

### Mass ownership and calculated scenarios

Fixed armour preserves the thirteen independently calculated legacy items. Each
owns a unique token and uses `area * thickness * 7.85 t/m3`. The 6,820.701 t
total is a bounding-box proxy, not a developed-plate weight. It includes fixed
belts, decks, bulkheads, four fixed barbettes and the conning tower. It excludes
rotating turret armour.

The four main-mount item uses `4 * 600 long ton` = 2,438.51258112 t. Its declared
calculation boundary owns the complete revolving mounting, rotating gunhouse
armour, and loading hoists above the fixed trunk. It excludes gun tubes,
ammunition and fixed barbettes. This interpretation and the use of Mark II mass
for Mark II* are assumptions. No separate turret-armour mass exists.

The machinery screening total is allocated 45% to boilers/uptakes and 55% to
turbines/shafts/condensers/auxiliaries. That allocation is an explicit
assumption. Fuel, working/feed water, lubricants and stores are excluded from
the installed-dry plant boundary. Fixed outfit is a 3% displacement screening
allowance with a 1.5-5% range. The torpedo launch outfit, secondary mounts and
torpedo rounds are declared placeholders with broad ranges.

The fixed/base nominal ledger is 26,021.630342730772 t. The fixture adds these
independent normal and deep engineering scenarios:

| Variable item | Normal (t) | Deep (t) | Basis |
| --- | ---: | ---: | --- |
| Coal | 900.000 | 3,600.000 | 25% and 100% of inherited maximum |
| Fuel oil | 292.500 | 1,170.000 | 25% and 100% of inherited maximum |
| Reserve/feed water | 411.498998064 | 685.83166344 | Exact 1.5% and 2.5% of `27000 * 1.0160469088 t` |
| Potable water | 70.000 | 178.500 | people × days × 5 kg/day scenario |
| Provisions/stores | 56.000 | 142.800 | people × days × 4 kg/day scenario |
| Crew/effects | 100.000 | 127.500 | people × 0.10 t scenario |
| **Predicted total** | **27,851.62934079477** | **31,926.26200617077** | fixed/base plus listed variables |

The deep overrides include scenario provenance. Their application clears the
base uncertainty intervals under the loading contract, so the fixture discloses
that scenario-specific intervals are incomplete. No residual, margin or
unallocated displacement item is present.

### Historical validation gaps

The following remain unresolved and prevent historical validation:

1. A builder or Admiralty weight return with category definitions and unit
   convention.
2. Mark II* mounting mass and the precise gun, armour, trunk and hoist boundary.
3. A Queen Mary fixed/rotating armour schedule with developed plate areas.
4. Installed-dry machinery weight and its water, lubricant, tool and stores
   boundary.
5. Dated normal/deep fuel, water, stores, crew and ammunition conditions.
6. Secondary mounting, torpedo tube/gear and torpedo outfit weights.
7. Weight-moment or inclining data for longitudinal and vertical centres.
8. A historical lines plan and actual watertight subdivision/opening schedule.

## Generic steamer reference

The generic steamer is a purpose-built analytic cargo vessel, not a historical
claim. Its 90 m × 13 m × 5.5 m hull uses `Cb = 0.72` and `Cwp = 0.82` with the
parameter-derived `geometry.make_reference_hull` construction. The resulting
section polygons are stored in the project, so consuming the saved project does
not call that function or read another case.

Its coastal loading totals 4,700 t from six independent analytic allocations.
The loaded condition changes fuel, water and cargo through ordinary loading
overrides. Armour, armament and ammunition are optional groups explicitly
absent from the unarmed fixture. Compartments and openings are synthetic
engineering fixtures.

## Analytic box reference

The box uses three explicit identical 10 m × 8 m rectangular sections over a
20 m length. At a 4 m waterline its volume is exactly `20 * 10 * 4 = 800 m3`;
at 1.025 t/m3 the corresponding reference mass is 820 t. The weight model is an
independent 700 t structure plus 120 t ballast. The light condition explicitly
sets ballast to known zero. All inapplicable optional groups state why they are
empty.

## Reproduction

Run the deterministic generator with the project interpreter:

```powershell
$env:PYTHONIOENCODING='utf-8'
& 'C:/Users/杨睿/.workbuddy/binaries/python/versions/3.13.12/python.exe' -B tools/plimsoll/tools/gen_project_cases.py
```

Generation uses only tracked JSON inputs, has no network or Blender dependency,
and records no current timestamp or absolute path. The two reference projects
are self-contained and contain no Queen Mary-specific values or file references.
